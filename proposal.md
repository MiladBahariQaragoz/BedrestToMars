From Bed Rest to Mars: Development of a Literature-Derived Machine Learning Framework for Predicting Lower-Limb Muscle Atrophy in Spaceflight Analogues
Introduction: Musculoskeletal deconditioning is a challenge during long-duration spaceflight. Head-down bed rest (HDBR) and prolonged bed rest are established terrestrial analogues of microgravity. This study aimed to develop a literature-derived dataset and machine-learning framework for predicting lower-limb muscle atrophy.
Methods: Data were extracted from 15 bed-rest and HDBR studies (1992–2025), covering unloading durations of 14–119 days. Variables included unloading duration, muscle group, participant characteristics, imaging modality, and exercise countermeasures. A comparative framework incorporating Linear Regression, Random Forest, Support Vector Regression, and Gradient Boosting was designed. Leave-One-Study-Out cross-validation and SHAP-based feature interpretation were incorporated.
Results: The dataset demonstrated a consistent relationship between unloading duration and muscle loss. Antigravity muscles, particularly the soleus, gastrocnemius, and triceps surae, showed the greatest susceptibility to unloading, with losses approaching 20–30% during prolonged bed rest. Exercise countermeasures were consistently associated with reduced muscle loss. Preliminary analyses identified unloading duration and muscle-specific vulnerability as major determinants of musculoskeletal deconditioning.
Conclusions: Literature-derived datasets provide a foundation for AI-assisted prediction of muscle atrophy in spaceflight analogues and may support future personalized countermeasures for lunar and Martian exploration missions.
